
Imagine you're playing a giant, super-detailed version of the video game *The Sims*.

1.  **Create the People:** Instead of just one family, you create a huge group of people - say, 100,000 of them. This is your starting population. You make them realistic, with different ages, genders, and backgrounds.

2.  **Live Life Year by Year:** You press "fast-forward" and the computer simulates their entire lives, year by year. In this simulation, real-life things can happen. People get older, they might get sick with a specific disease, they might get better, and eventually, they pass away. Everything happens based on probabilities, just like in the real world.

3.  **Test an Idea:** Now, the real reason for this simulation is to test out new ideas for healthcare. Let's say you have a new intervention that could help people with that disease.

4.  **Run Two Experiments:**
    *   **Run #1 (No New Medicine):** You run the entire simulation for all 100,000 people and see what happens. How many get sick? How much does their healthcare cost over their lifetime? How much "quality of life" do they have?
    *   **Run #2 (With New Medicine):** You hit "reset" and run the *exact same simulation*, but this time, you give the new medicine to the people who need it.

5.  **Compare the Results:** When it's all done, you compare the two outcomes. Did the new medicine help people live longer, healthier lives? How much extra did it cost?

**The bottom line:** This project is like a crystal ball for health decisions. It lets us see the possible future of a new treatment on a computer, helping us decide if it is worth the money and effort in the real world before we spend millions of dollars to find out.
